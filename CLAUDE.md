# Notes for Claude

## Prototype version number

The prototype shows its version in the bottom-left corner: the `.proto-ver` element in `src/prototype.html` (its text and its `aria-label`). Format: `vMAJOR.MINOR` or `vMAJOR.MINOR.PATCH`.

Every update the user asks for bumps the version before it is committed and published. Claude decides whether the update is small or bigger:

- **Small → patch** (v1.3 → v1.3.1 → v1.3.2): tweaks and fixes to what already exists, such as copy edits, spacing, sizes, colours, alignment, a bug fix, or polishing one element.
- **Bigger → minor** (v1.3.2 → v1.4): new or reworked behaviour or layout, such as a new screen, state, variant, feature or interaction, a restructured layout, or a change that spans several screens. The patch number is dropped: v1.4, not v1.4.0. After v1.9 comes v1.10.
- **Major** (v1.x → v2.0): only when the user explicitly says so (e.g. "increase to v2"). Minor and patch reset: v2.0.

Changes that don't touch the prototype itself (README, these notes) don't bump the version.

Start the commit subject with the new version (e.g. `v1.3.1: …`) and mention it in the reply.

## Variant C visibility

Variant C ("picture on top") can be hidden without removing it. `SHOW_C` in `src/prototype.html` controls it: `true` shows its button in the More options panel (bottom-right corner, Flows view) and lets `#c` open it (current state); `false` hides that row and sends `#c` links and a remembered C choice to A, while all of C's markup, styles and logic stay in place. When hiding it, also add a "hidden for now" note to C in the README; when showing it, remove that note.

## Hidden decorations in the 3D look

The 3D look's resume fragments (the four faded pages in the side margins of the method screen) are hidden, not removed. `SHOW_3D_PAPERS = false` in `src/prototype.html` adds `no-papers` to the backdrop, which hides them; their markup and styles stay in place. To bring them back, set `SHOW_3D_PAPERS = true` (and update the README's look description); nothing else needs to change.

## Upload modal or building screen

The user's rule: a resume that is **uploaded or imported** (Upload a resume, Import from LinkedIn, the Upload resume and LinkedIn import screens) goes through the **upload modal** and then straight to the destination or the next step; one that is **created** (Write with AI, Paste text, a section writer) goes through the **building screen** and then the same way. `startCreation` splits them. The modal is from Figma (Kickresume 3.0 › upload modals, 62245:57072): `UPM` holds each version's picture (`assets/upload/`, 2x exports of the Figma frames) and line, `UPM_FOR` picks the version by the URL of what the resume is for (the screen that asked, `cvNext` + `routeNow`, else the run's feature), anything else gets Resume Upload only (`upload`); `UPM_RUN` the titles (Uploading → Importing → Finishing upload; LinkedIn starts at Importing, with its own note), `UPM_FAIL` Upload failed.'s message (Error is Figma's "The server is taking too long to respond."). `upmHTML` / `runUpm` are the card and its states (`data-state` run, done, fail; More options › Result decides, as for the tailoring card), `upmCreate` shows it on the method screen in the methods' place (`#create-upm`; `renderCreate` puts the methods back), `upmRoute` in the card's place on a route screen (the screen's headline stays, × hides). The ATS check has no loading of its own: after the CV it opens the ATS checker. Jobs for You keeps its own upload and Analyzing step (Flow 1 board); the Figma file's Job Matcher and Pyjama Jobs versions aren't used yet.

## Route screens (recreated product screens)

The user sends screenshots of product screens (mostly modals) to recreate pixel for pixel, each under a URL path such as `/dashboard/create-ai-resume/` or `/ai-career-map`. They live in `src/prototype.html`:

- Register each one in `ROUTES` (`group` = its group in the user's URL checklist (kept as data; the URLs list is grouped by `KINDS`), `path`, `also` = more paths for the same screen, `title`, `html` = its markup function, `init` = its behaviour, `needsCv` = without a CV, the method screen comes first and hands the CV to this screen once it is made (`askCvFor` / `askCv` set `cvNext`; the upload modal's Continue or the building screen's end (`buildNext`) runs it; the URL stays the screen's; see "Upload modal or building screen"), `upload` = the screen is Upload your resume itself, then the upload modal and the editor, `wire` = no design yet: its `html` returns a Wire wireframe from `WIRE`, `run` = do something instead of showing a screen, e.g. open the editor, `more` = panes that follow the main one, `{ name: markup function }`, such as Tailor resume's `process`, `head` = the screen's headline and subheadline per pane, `{ pane: [headline, subheadline] }` or a function giving them, shown above the card like the onboarding screens' `.page-head` (`routeHead` / `setRouteHead`; nested steps such as a writer's `s2` can have their own). Every personalization, processing and processing-state screen has one, so its content starts where the goal, method and building screens' does: write the copy in `HEADS` (wireframes opened by `wireRoute` get `HEADS[<WIRE id>]`, and their card drops the title the headline now carries)). That alone adds its link under More options › URLs and makes `#<path>` open it (also on `hashchange`).
- A screenshot of a whole page with its own top bar (Sign up and Log in, `openAuth`; My Documents, `openDocs`; the resume tools, `openTool`; the template picker, `openTemplates`) or its own background (the purple Upgrade and student pages, `openUpgrade`) can't sit in `#screen-route`: it is an app screen like the dashboard (`APPS.auth`, `APPS.upgrade`, `APPS.docs`, `APPS.tool`, `APPS.tpl`, which hide the logo bar and page) and its route uses `run`. `enterApp` opens one; give it the URL it shows. Images live in `assets/`; write `__ASSET:<path under assets/>__` where the page needs one and `build.py` inlines it as a data URI (the auth photos still use their own placeholders).
- A design may come as a Figma link instead of a screenshot. Read it with the Figma MCP tools (`get_design_context`, `get_metadata`, `get_screenshot` at `maxDimension` 1440 for the compare). When www.figma.com is blocked for downloads, export assets through `use_figma` (`exportAsync` as SVG strings, or PNG/WebP bytes base64-encoded in chunks under its 48 KB return limit). Figma's code export can flatten a gradient stroke to its first colour: sample the screenshot (the Upgrade card and the ISIC tab borders are gradients). The local `dist` file has no doctype (quirks mode) while the published artifact does, so set margins on `p`/`form` explicitly and compare a copy with `<!doctype html>` prepended.
- Steps inside a screen (upload → tool, step 1 → step 2) are `.rt-pane`s switched with `showPane(root, name, dir)`; a height change inside a card uses `morphHeight`. Reuse `uploadHTML`/`initUpload` (a valid file goes to the upload modal, `upmRoute`), `selectHTML`/`refreshSel`, `langHTML`/`initLang`, `needFilled` and the `.rt-btn` / `.rt-ai` / `.rt-coral` / `.rt-ghost` buttons. A card has no × of its own (even when the screenshot shows one; the tailoring card's × on its error state, back to the form, is part of the processing): the screen's corner × (`#top-x`, `syncTopX`) shows on a route's steps that have a headline, except processing, results and destinations (`process`, `progress`, `state`, `dest` panes) and routes with `noX` (the sign-up wall, and the job board, which has the design's Back under it like the method screen).
- The screenshots carry the Mac's display-P3 colour profile: convert sampled colours to sRGB before using them (the coral then reads exactly `#ff5e59`, the AI gradient `#5345ff → #9b3eff`). Check new class names against existing ones (`.mini` and `.lbl` were already taken).
- They are full screens, never pop-ups with an overlay (the user asked for this to keep the flow unified): `openRoute` fills `#screen-route` inside the page and moves there with `go('route')`, so they get the logo bar, page background, top line and slide transitions of the other screens. A modal's card keeps its inside exactly as in the screenshot, but its surface matches the goal and method cards (1px `--kr-grey-60` outline, `--radius-card` corners, no drop shadow; reduce the padding by 1px for the outline). Wrap animated content in `.rt-item`.
- Keyboard and screen readers: a screen or step change sends focus left on something now hidden to the new view's headline (`landFocus`, called by `go`, `showPane`, `enterApp`, `openRoute` and the app screens); groups of `role="radio"` buttons get one Tab stop and arrow keys from one shared handler (give the checked one `tabindex="0"`, the others `-1`); a dropdown that closes on `focusout` uses `closeOnLeave` (Safari doesn't focus a clicked button).
- Give each screen its own class prefix (the AI screen uses `.aim-*`) and its own CSS block under "Route screens".
- Match the screenshots exactly: measure them (they are usually 2x), use the product's sizes rather than the onboarding's height-aware spacing, and compare a 2x render against the screenshot pixel by pixel before publishing.
- Later the user will say which buttons (goal screen, method screen, dashboard) lead to which route; wire those with `openRoute(findRoute(path))`.

## The six groups

Both link lists (More options › Flows' Open and the URLs view) are grouped by `KINDS`, which the user chose: what a feature takes in and gives back, so the features in one group ask for the same data, load the same way and end in the same kind of place. Get a resume (the CV is the result: method screen, templates, samples, AI, upload, LinkedIn, guest editor), Improve my resume (CV required, a short setup, its own loading, back in the editor: Tailor, Rewrite, Summary, the section writers), Check my resume (CV required, no setup or loading, a report page: ATS, Proofreading, Translate, Recruiter feedback, Career coach and their latest-resume links), Write something new (CV optional, a short form, a new document: Cover letter, Interview questions, Resignation letter, Career Map, cover letter and website templates), Find jobs (the jobs pages) and Just open (a page: Sign up, Log in, Dashboard, My Documents, Editor, Latest resume, Upgrade). Every URL is listed in exactly one (`kindOf`; the order within a group is the list's, `kindAt`); put a new URL in its group when adding it.

## The app menu

Every page inside the app uses the editor's left menu (`appNavHTML(at)`: `.ed-side`, and `.ed-mbar` on phones), never a page's own top bar or side menu from a design; `at` marks the page. Its links carry `data-nav` (one click handler opens the page). Put it first in the page's grid (column 1); `enterApp` and `openDashboard` keep it in place when moving between app pages.

- The resume tools with nothing to ask (`TOOL_PAGE`; no setup screen) are `run` routes: `analyseNow` (the ATS checker, with no loading of its own: the user's rule) and `toolNow` (`openTool(kind, path)` under the tool's own URL; without a CV the method screen first). ## Wireframes and flow templates

- Screens without a design are wireframes built with Wire (the `anthropic-skills:wire-wireframes` skill; its CSS is copied in with tokens scoped to `.wf`). Write them as `WIRE.<id>` markup functions using Wire classes only; buttons carry `data-wire` (they move a running flow on, otherwise do the obvious thing). Keep them shades of grey only (Wire's accent, status and note tokens are set to greys). A screen's note goes in `wNote(text)`: it is kept out of the screen and shown in the chrome note at the bottom (`syncNote`), so it never reads as part of the design. When the user sends the real design, replace the route's `html` with a recreated screen and drop `wire`.
## The four flows

- Every URL follows one of four flow templates (`FLOW_T`, from the user's flow board in Figma): 1 intent collection → feature personalization → data processing → processing state → destination (Skip personalization → dashboard); 2 personalization → processing → state → destination; 3 processing → state → destination; 4 destination. Sign up, Log in or a landing page can come first (`entry`). The user's idea: a new URL only pairs to one of the four and fills in its own screens. The user's rules: Flow 3 is only for input given on a landing page, a CV (`landing-cv`, `landing-ats`) or, for tailoring, a CV and the tailoring setup (`landing-tailor`); its CV is set (`cv: true`) and goes through the upload modal first. A URL that carries its input itself (a template or sample) is Flow 4. Translate resume (Flow 4) never runs without a CV (`cv: true`).
- `FEATURES` pairs every URL with its flow: `flow`, `group` (the URL checklist's group, kept as data), `title`, `path` (`url` when the shown URL has a placeholder such as `{template}`), `entry`, `prep` (the feature personalization), `process` (default: the shared processing wireframe worded by `verb` and `psteps`; `false`: none, and no processing state either, like the job board's search), `dest` (the destination), `noun` (what the result is called), `cv` (start with or without a CV; without it the panel's CV choice decides, `pmCvOn` / `runsWithCv`), `guest` (adds the sign-up wall after the result), `state` (the feature's own processing state instead of the shared `WIRE.state`), `redo` (Flow 3: the feature whose run "Change the input" switches to, since the input came before the run). `prep`, `process` and `dest` are steps from the `S.*` helpers (`wire`, `route`, `wireTool`, `cv` (a tool's CV: the method screen; `toolPrep`), `upm` / `buildFor` (the CV made there: the upload modal or the building screen, both in `cvMade(path)`; a run goes on from whichever the person took), `trlRun` (Tailor's card as processing and state), `screen`, `build`, `editor`, `dash`, `docs`, `tool`, `auth`, `upgrade`, `latest`, `jobs`, `dest`), or a function returning them when they depend on the CV (`toolPrep`). `buildFlow(f)` lays them out in the flow's stages. To add a URL, add a `FEATURES` entry (and a `ROUTES` entry if its screen should be in the URLs list).
- Processing moves on by itself (`processDone`; the building screen through `afterBuild`, which hands over to the run only when the building screen is its processing; the upload modal waits for Continue, `upmDone`, which goes to the screen that asked (`cvNext`), else the run's next step (skipping the building screen, its `alt`, and result steps) or the editor; Back after a failed upload in a run with no personalization (Flow 3) starts the `redo` feature). The Upload and LinkedIn import screens' runs use `upmRun(path)`: the modal as processing (`S.route(…, 'progress')`) and its result as the processing state (`'progress-end'`). More options › Result sets `procResult`: Completed skips the processing state and goes straight to the destination; Failed and Error show it (`WIRE.state`), with a way back to the personalization (`redo` for Flow 3) or the processing; a run's building screen set to fail stops early without celebrating (`runBuilding`). Generate work experience / objective are section writers: `SECTION_AS` words their building screen, `withSection` changes only that section when there is a CV, and they end in the editor at that section (`openEditorAt`; a dest step can do the same with `S.editor(label, path, sec)`).
- Flow 1's goal screen switches the run to the goal picked (`flowGoal`). A screen's own button moves a run on with `flowOn()` / `flowNext()`.
- The More options panel has two views (`setPmTab`): Flows and URLs (a CV switch, `[data-cv]` / `setCv`, for the screens opened from there, then `#pm-routes`, every `ROUTES` entry). Flows is one card that works like the app will (the user's idea: the URL says where the person ends up, a resolver shows only the steps whose data is missing): Open (`pmKey`, a feature's `path`; `PM_URLS`, one entry per URL in the six `KINDS` groups, in its own listbox `.pmf-dd`: fixed-position, scrolls inside itself, `ddOpen` / `ddClose` / `ddActive` / `pmPick`; never the native select, which fills a Mac's screen), From (`pmFrom`: `signup`, `app`, `lp`, `guest`, from `fromOf(f)`: Flow 1 features are Sign up, `landing-*` and Translate are Landing page, guest features Guest, the rest App; choices a URL doesn't have are dimmed) and CV (`pmCvOn`; dimmed when the URL's entries for that arrival decide it, `pmCvFree`). `pmFeature()` resolves them to one `FEATURES` entry; the card shows its flow and stages with Run (`data-feature`, `startFeature`), the URL and steps (`pmSteps`) folded under Steps. `renderFlowsPanel` draws the card once and `pmUpdate` changes it in place (no redraws: the panel must not jump). The user wants it minimal: only what matters on load, no intro text. Starting or switching a run (`startFeature`, `flowGoal`) moves the card to it (`pmShow`). Every feature must stay reachable from the card: one entry per path, arrival and CV. Result and Look sit under the card. The Flow checker switch above it (`checkerOn`, remembered as `kr-flow-checker`; `setChecker`, `startChecker`) starts a Flow 1 run on Sign up; `checkerAdopt(path)` (from `setHash`, and `openDashboard`) makes whatever the person opens in the app its own App run once the current run has none of that screen or has reached its destination (and not for the dashboard after Skip personalization); Restart and loading start on Sign up while it's on. The bar groups the run's dots by stage.
